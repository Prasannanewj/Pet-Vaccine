# Pet Vaccine Promotions Platform — Product Strategy

**Executive review | November 2026**  
**Planning scope:** U.S. canine influenza vaccine marketing promotions, clinic participation, and owner engagement.  
**Status:** Strategic recommendation for discussion; not an approved Zoetis operating plan, clinical recommendation, or financial commitment.

## 1. Vision statement

Every dog whose veterinarian recommends protection can be informed, connected to a participating veterinary clinic, and supported through the appropriate vaccination course—through trusted, measurable, and responsible engagement.

## 2. Product mission

Help Zoetis and veterinary practices turn approved vaccine education and offers into more informed owner conversations, easier clinic participation, and more veterinarian-recommended vaccination courses completed. Make the clinic the center of the experience, preserve the veterinarian’s clinical judgment, and measure incremental outcomes rather than clicks or coupon volume alone.

## 3. Strategic themes

1. **Veterinary-led, owner-friendly education.** Explain canine influenza and exposure considerations in plain language; direct owners to a veterinarian for individual recommendations.
2. **Clinic participation with low operational friction.** Give clinics simple onboarding, approved materials, offer setup, staff guidance, and visibility into campaign activity.
3. **Relevant, responsible promotions.** Make offers transparent, easy to understand and redeem, limited to approved terms, and subordinate to clinical appropriateness.
4. **Connected engagement across channels.** Coordinate clinic, field-team, email, web, referral, and reminder touchpoints with consent, suppression, and frequency controls.
5. **Incrementality and accountability.** Use clinic- or cohort-level comparisons to distinguish genuinely additional recommended vaccinations from activity that would have occurred anyway.
6. **Trust, safety, and compliance by design.** Use approved claims and assets, minimum necessary data, clear consent, and auditable campaign approvals.

## 4. Business objectives

- Increase awareness of canine influenza and support informed veterinarian conversations among owners whose dogs may have relevant exposure.
- Recruit and activate clinics and other approved facility partners; reduce the time and effort needed to participate.
- Increase redemption of eligible, approved promotions while measuring their incremental effect on completed vaccinations.
- Improve follow-through on veterinarian-recommended vaccine schedules and reminders, where applicable to the product label and clinical plan.
- Improve Zoetis and clinic visibility into campaign reach, engagement, referral, redemption, and vaccination outcomes.
- Build a reusable promotions capability that can be governed and adapted for future products only after product-specific clinical, regulatory, and business review.

## 5. Customer segments

| Segment | Need / opportunity |
|---|---|
| Dog owners with higher exposure opportunities | Understand risk factors and know how to discuss protection with their veterinarian; examples include boarding, daycare, grooming, travel, and group activities. Exposure does not itself establish that vaccination is appropriate. |
| Veterinary practices and clinic teams | Explain recommendations, respond to owner questions, and make offers or follow-up easy to administer without adding substantial staff workload. |
| Clinic managers and practice groups | Coordinate staff, inventory, local outreach, participation terms, and performance across one or more locations. |
| Zoetis field and marketing teams | Deliver consistent, approved campaigns; support clinics; learn which channels and offers produce incremental outcomes. |
| Approved community/facility partners | Share veterinarian-approved information and refer owners to participating clinics without making clinical claims or recommendations. |

## 6. User personas

- **Pet owner — “Maya”:** A dog owner who boards or attends daycare. She wants concise, credible information, a nearby clinic, transparent offer terms, and a convenient next step. She does not want a promotion to substitute for veterinary advice.
- **Veterinarian — “Dr. Lee”:** A clinician who needs accurate, label-consistent educational material and an efficient way to discuss an individual dog’s exposure and vaccination options. Clinical recommendation remains theirs.
- **Clinic manager — “Jordan”:** Runs a busy practice and needs quick registration, clear staff instructions, simple redemption, and useful local reporting without extra manual reconciliation.
- **Zoetis campaign manager — “Alex”:** Plans and measures compliant campaigns across channels and needs controlled content, audience rules, approval history, and reliable performance data.

## 7. Market challenges

- **Risk is individual and contextual.** Canine influenza vaccination is not a blanket recommendation for every dog; AAHA guidance emphasizes individualized vaccine decisions and lifestyle/risk factors. The platform must inform a veterinary discussion rather than decide eligibility.
- **Owners may underestimate exposure.** Group settings and contact with other dogs can be relevant, but education must avoid fear-based or unsubstantiated urgency.
- **Clinic time and systems are fragmented.** Practices vary in staffing, digital tools, inventory workflows, and appetite for new portals. A technically sound offer can still fail if staff cannot explain or process it easily.
- **Promotions can distort measurement.** Redemptions may be misattributed, shared, or used by owners who would have vaccinated anyway; redemption alone is not proof of incremental demand.
- **Claims and offers require governance.** Veterinary biologics are regulated, and product claims, labels, and promotional materials require appropriate regulatory and legal review. Requirements may vary by channel and jurisdiction.
- **Competitive messages are evolving.** New product technologies and formulations increase the need for precise, current, evidence-based communication rather than generic superiority claims.
- **Data is distributed.** Owner consent, clinic systems, campaign platforms, and vaccination records may not share identifiers or definitions; measurement must work with approved, minimized data.

Public product pages and announcements do not establish current market share, local availability, pricing, or comparative clinical outcomes; validate these with current market research before investment decisions.

**Recommended position:** Differentiate the platform as the most dependable clinic-first way to deliver approved education and promotions, coordinate participation, and measure incremental veterinarian-recommended outcomes—not as a mechanism to make vaccine decisions for owners.

## 9. Customer problems to solve

- Owners cannot easily tell whether their dog’s lifestyle warrants a conversation with a veterinarian or where to have it.
- Clinics lack a consistent, low-effort way to access approved materials, enroll in a campaign, explain offer terms, and refer owners.
- Managers have limited visibility into offer eligibility, redemption, staff execution, and campaign performance.
- Marketing teams cannot reliably connect exposure and redemption to completed, appropriate vaccination activity or compare channels fairly.
- Owners who consent to follow-up may miss an agreed next step because reminders and preferences are scattered across systems.

## 10. Value proposition

- **For owners:** Clear, trustworthy education, a simple route to a participating clinic, transparent promotional terms, and optional follow-up they control.
- **For veterinarians:** Clinically governed materials and an easier conversation—not automated diagnosis, pressure, or replacement of professional judgment.
- **For clinic managers:** Faster enrollment, ready-to-use assets, simple redemption, staff support, and actionable reporting.
- **For Zoetis:** A governed omnichannel operating layer that links education, clinic participation, approved offers, and outcome measurement while producing learnings for future campaigns.

## 11. Key capabilities

1. **Clinic and partner management:** Applications, verification, location profiles, campaign enrollment, staff resources, and partner support.
2. **Content and claims governance:** Version-controlled approved assets, audience/channel usage rules, expiration dates, approval workflows, and audit history.
3. **Offer management:** Configurable dates, eligibility, geographies, participating clinics, limits, disclosures, and redemption rules; no offer goes live without required approvals.
4. **Owner education and clinic discovery:** Mobile-first product education, clear “talk to your veterinarian” calls to action, participating-clinic search, and contact/booking links where supported.
5. **Referral and redemption:** Clinic-specific or campaign-specific codes, transparent terms, fraud/leakage controls, and a simple clinic workflow.
6. **Consent-based reminders:** Preference capture, opt-in and opt-out, frequency controls, suppression, and reminders only for a veterinarian-agreed schedule and applicable label.
7. **Measurement and experimentation:** Reach, engagement, qualified clinic actions, referrals, verified redemptions, completed vaccination outcomes where authorized, holdouts, and cost per incremental outcome.
8. **Operational analytics:** Clinic dashboards, campaign summaries, data-gap flags, approved exports, and role-based access.

## 12. North Star metric

**Incremental veterinarian-recommended, label-concordant canine influenza vaccination courses completed among eligible dogs at participating clinics, versus a pre-specified matched or randomized comparison group.**

Count an outcome only when the clinic or another approved source verifies administration, deduplicates it, and the data use is authorized. Do not count clicks, referrals, or coupon claims as vaccinations. The measure must not reward vaccination where the veterinarian has not recommended it.

## 13. Product success metrics

Set final targets after baseline collection, pilot design, and statistical-power review. The ranges below are **first-year hypotheses for leadership validation**, not commitments.

| Outcome | Metric / proposed first-year signal |
|---|---|
| Incremental clinical outcome | Aim for a positive, statistically credible lift in verified, veterinarian-recommended completed courses versus control; test a 10% relative lift as an initial hypothesis. |
| Clinic participation | Track invited-to-registered, registered-to-campaign-active, and repeat participation rates; establish a baseline and improve activation each quarter. |
| Owner progression | Measure educational visits progressing to a clinic locator, contact, or booking action; compare by channel and eligible cohort rather than optimizing raw traffic. |
| Promotion effectiveness | Report eligible redemption and incremental completed courses per redemption; monitor offer leakage, duplicate use, and cost per incremental outcome. |
| Follow-through | Measure consented reminder delivery, opt-out, and completion of the veterinarian-agreed next step; never use reminder engagement as a proxy for clinical suitability. |
| Clinic experience | Track setup time, support requests, staff-reported friction, and repeat campaign use. Reduce operational burden from pilot baseline. |
| Campaign efficiency | Cost per incremental completed recommended course, channel contribution, and time from campaign approval to launch. |
| Guardrails | Zero unapproved claims or unauthorized audience use; monitor complaints, unsubscribe rates, redemption abuse, privacy/security incidents, and any signal of inappropriate pressure or vaccination. Stop or correct campaigns when a guardrail is breached. |

## 14. Product risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Owner-facing promotion is interpreted as a medical recommendation or guarantee | Route individual decisions to the veterinarian; use approved language, prominent terms, and clinical/legal review. Avoid fear-based urgency and unsupported effectiveness claims. |
| Offers drive inappropriate demand or undermine trust | Do not infer clinical eligibility from browsing, exposure, or AI scores. Require veterinarian recommendation and monitor complaints and clinic feedback. |
| Unapproved or outdated content reaches a channel | Central asset library, versioning, expiry controls, required sign-offs, and takedown capability. |
| Redemption rises without incremental vaccination | Use matched or randomized holdouts, verify administration where authorized, deduplicate, and report redemption separately from outcomes. |
| Clinic workload or technology integration prevents adoption | Co-design with clinics, keep initial workflows lightweight, support multiple redemption paths, train staff, and provide field support. |
| Personal data is used beyond consent or retained too long | Data minimization, purpose limitation, clear consent and preferences, role-based access, retention/deletion rules, privacy review, and auditable exports. |
| Local outbreaks or competitor claims make content stale | Use approved, dated sources and rapid review; prohibit unverified outbreak notices and unapproved comparative claims. |
| AI creates biased, inaccurate, or overly persuasive messages | Human-reviewed, approved templates; constrained use; no autonomous clinical advice or eligibility decisions; monitor outputs and provide rollback. |

## 15. Assumptions to validate

- Initial launch is U.S.-focused and centers on canine influenza vaccine promotion, consistent with the repository’s Pet-Vaccine positioning and existing issues.
- The platform is an engagement and campaign capability, not a veterinary record system or clinical decision-support product.
- Participating clinics can support a simple owner referral or redemption workflow; integrations are optional for the first pilot.
- Zoetis clinical, regulatory, legal, privacy, security, and commercial teams will define approved claims, offer rules, data uses, and required channel reviews before launch.
- Clinic participation, baseline vaccination activity, offer economics, channel costs, local availability, and market size are unknown and must be researched rather than assumed.
- All proposed performance targets are hypotheses. Use pilot data and an agreed comparison design to set scale thresholds.
- Product labels, availability, guidelines, and competitor information can change; revalidate before publishing external content.

## 17. AI and personalization opportunities

Prioritize safe, bounded assistance that improves relevance or operations without making clinical decisions:

- **Channel and timing optimization:** Recommend campaign timing and channel based on aggregate, consented engagement and clinic capacity; provide a human-readable rationale and controls.
- **Content adaptation:** Draft approved-template variants for clinic, owner, or partner audiences; only publish after human and required regulatory review. Keep claims locked to approved source content.
- **Clinic insights:** Summarize campaign performance, surface missing data, and flag unusual redemption patterns for human investigation.
- **Owner journey personalization:** Use voluntarily supplied preferences and broad context (such as selected activity interests) to tailor educational pathways and clinic discovery—not to diagnose, infer disease, or determine vaccine eligibility.
- **Reminder orchestration:** Send only opted-in, veterinarian-agreed reminders using approved schedules; support frequency caps, quiet hours, and immediate preference changes.

Do not use AI to provide veterinary advice, create unreviewed vaccine claims, infer medical eligibility, generate outbreak alerts without verified sources, or optimize toward vaccination volume without clinical guardrails. Evaluate model quality, bias, privacy, explainability, and human override before deployment.

## 18. Dependencies

- **Clinical and Medical Affairs:** Clinical accuracy, risk framing, owner education, and veterinary workflow review.
- **Regulatory and Legal:** Current product-label alignment, biologics advertising/promotional review, state/local offer rules, disclosures, and terms.
- **Privacy, Security, and Data Governance:** Consent, data minimization, retention, access, vendor review, incident response, and approved measurement design.
- **Veterinary clinics and field teams:** Co-design, clinic recruitment, training, local support, and verified outcome capture.
- **Digital Product, Engineering, and UX:** Mobile web, clinic tools, identity/permissions, content workflow, integrations, reliability, accessibility, and analytics.
- **Marketing Operations and CRM:** Campaign coordination, suppression rules, channel orchestration, customer preferences, and approved asset distribution.
- **Analytics and Finance:** Baselines, holdouts, attribution, incremental lift, offer economics, cost per outcome, and scale thresholds.
- **Customer support and partner operations:** Owner/clinic escalation paths, FAQ maintenance, enrollment support, and issue resolution.

## 19. Executive summary

Build a clinic-first promotions platform that connects trusted canine-influenza education to participating clinics, transparent approved offers, consent-based follow-up, and credible outcome measurement. The central strategic choice is to optimize for **incremental veterinarian-recommended vaccination courses completed**, not awareness impressions, redemptions, or raw campaign volume.

Start with a governed U.S. pilot: establish baselines, co-design the workflow with clinics, release the minimum viable capabilities, and test against an appropriate comparison group. Scale only when the platform demonstrates incremental value, acceptable clinic burden, sound economics, and no compromise to clinical judgment, trust, privacy, or compliance. The existing repository Epics provide a practical delivery backbone for this strategy.
